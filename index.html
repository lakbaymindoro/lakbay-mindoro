About us / About the destination

An island of two worlds

Mindoro — “Mina de Oro” (gold mine) — pairs UNESCO marine biodiversity with sacred mountains and living Mangyan culture, just 2 hours from Manila.

Our background & mission

Lakbay Mindoro Travel & Tours was founded in 2016 in Calapan City, Oriental Mindoro, by a team of PADI dive masters, mountaineers, and Mangyan youth advocates. What began as weekend White Beach island-hopping is now the island's most-reviewed operator (4.9★ across 2,300+ Google & TripAdvisor reviews), serving 12,500+ guests from 40 countries.

Mission

To make Mindoro the Philippines' model for community-led, reef-safe tourism — where every trip funds conservation and indigenous livelihood.

Vision

A Mindoro where travelers, Mangyan communities, and marine ecosystems thrive together by 2030.

History & tourism significance

  • 1973 — Puerto Galera declared a UNESCO Man & Biosphere Reserve.One of the most biodiverse marine channels on Earth; center of the Coral Triangle.
  • 1996 — Apo Reef Natural Park protected.The country's largest contiguous coral reef (34 sq km lagoon), home to sharks, turtles & 400+ coral species.
  • 2016 — Lakbay Mindoro founded.First operator to bundle sea + summit + culture in single itineraries.
  • 2023 — Philippine Tourism Award finalist.Recognized for Mangyan fair-pay cultural tours & zero-plastic boat operations.

Location & uniqueness

Mindoro Island (10,245 km²) sits off Batangas, split into Oriental Mindoro (capital Calapan) and Occidental Mindoro (capital Mamburao). Nowhere else in Luzon can you dive a world-class reef in the morning and stand beneath 2,582-m Mt. Halcon — the country's most challenging climb — by the next sunrise. Add 7 distinct Mangyan ethnolinguistic groups with living suyat script and nito weaving.

  • • Transparent all-in pricing (permits, fees, gear — no surprise “environmental fees”)
  • • Max 12 guests; private tours at group prices for 6+

Travel information / Tourist guide

Travel smart, travel kind

Best time to visit

Nov–May (dry season) is ideal: calm seas for Apo Reef (Mar–May only; park closed Jun–Sep), clear Halcon trails Dec–Apr. Jun–Oct brings habagat swells — Galera stays open but expect choppy crossings and possible trip reshuffles. Whale shark season: Feb–Apr in Puerto Galera channel.

Weather: 26–32°C lowlands · 14–20°C on Halcon summit. Afternoon showers common; always pack a dry bag (we lend them free).

What to bring

  • Reef-safe sunscreen, rash guard, aqua shoes
  • Valid ID + dive certification card (if diving)
  • 2L water bottle (refill stations on all boats)
  • Light rain shell Nov–Feb; headlamp for Halcon
  • Cash: ATMs sparse in Sablayan; GCash widely accepted

Dress code

Beachwear is fine on the sand; cover shoulders/knees in town, churches & Mangyan villages. No camouflage swimwear (local ordinance near ports).

Safety reminders

  • Always wear life vests on island-hopping (provided, sanitized).
  • Dive only with our briefed masters; 24-hr no-fly rule enforced.
  • Halcon requires DENR permit + guide ratio 1:5; no solo treks.
  • Drink only treated/bottled water; we carry first-aid + oxygen on dive boats.

Local customs

Ask before photographing Mangyan elders; remove hats in homes; haggle kindly at markets. A simple “Salamat po” goes far.

Environmental guidelines

  • Take only photos — corals, shells & sand stay on the island.
  • Use provided reef-safe sunscreen; ordinary sunscreen banned at Apo Reef.
  • Segregate trash; every boat carries color-coded bins.
  • Keep 5 m from turtles & 10 m from whale sharks. Never touch.
